  "weapon_phrase": string or null — a verbatim substring from the narrative that justifies the weapon classification. MUST appear exactly in the narrative (character for character). Null if weapon is "unknown".

Classification guidance:
  weapon:
    firearm      — any gun, shot, bullet, shooting, gunshot wounds
    knife        — cutting / stabbing instruments
    blunt        — club, hammer, pipe, bludgeon, or any NAMED blunt object.
                   "Beaten" or "beating" ALONE is NOT blunt — see personal.
    personal     — beaten by hand/fist/foot, stomped, kicked (NOT strangulation).
                   Any "beaten" / "beating" narrative with NO named implement
                   is personal, not blunt. Blunt requires an object.
    asphyxiation — strangled, suffocated, drowned, ligature (anything that cuts off air)
    fire         — arson, set on fire, burned as cause
    other        — any other specified weapon (poison, vehicle as weapon, etc.)
    unknown      — narrative doesn't say or is ambiguous

  location_type (walk this decision tree TOP DOWN; STOP at the first YES;
  check each question in order, do not skip ahead):

    Step 1. Is the victim INSIDE a car or truck at the time of the violent
            event? Triggers: "driving his vehicle", "in the back seat",
            "slumped in the driver's seat", "shot through the window"
            while in the car, "pulled over", "riding in the car".
              YES → vehicle. STOP. (Do not proceed even if the car is
              parked at a store, on a road, in a lot, etc.)
              NO  → go to Step 2.

    Step 2. Is the violent event AT the dwelling itself or its PRIVATE
            curtilage (the home's own enclosed/attached spaces)? Triggers:
            "inside her home", "in his apartment", "at the residence of",
            "in the (fenced) backyard", "in the driveway", "on the front
            porch", "in the attached garage", "in the shed on the property".
            A bare street address is GEOLOCATION, not a scene type: a body in
            a public or natural outdoor feature described relative to an
            address (see Step 4) is NOT residence.
              YES → residence. STOP.
              NO  → go to Step 3.

    Step 3. Is the event INSIDE a non-residential building?
              3a. Customer-facing business (store, bank, gas station,
                  hotel, motel, restaurant, retail shop, convenience
                  store)? This applies even if the victim was an employee.
                    YES → commercial. STOP.
              3b. Non-customer-facing occupational site (construction
                  site, warehouse, private office, industrial facility)?
                    YES → workplace. STOP.
              3c. Some other enclosed non-residential structure?
                    YES → other. STOP.
              If NO to all of 3a/3b/3c → go to Step 4.

    Step 4. Is the event OUTDOORS in a public or natural feature: a road,
            street, intersection, sidewalk, alley, parking lot (not attached
            to a residence), park, field, woods, ravine, ditch, creek, or an
            open grass area? This holds EVEN IF a street address is given to
            locate it — "in the grass in front of <address>", "in a creek
            behind <address>", "in the street outside <address>", "in a field
            near <address>" are all public_outdoor (the address geolocates the
            body; the scene is the outdoor feature). Also: "near" or "in the
            area of" a named venue but actually on a road or in a public area
            → still public_outdoor.
              YES → public_outdoor. STOP.

    Step 5. If the narrative does not state a location or is fully
            ambiguous → unknown.
